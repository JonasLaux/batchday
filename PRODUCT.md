# Batchday

A private, single-user meal planner for Austrian grocery shopping, running locally or on the owner's Node homeserver. Application code is open source under MIT; each installation's recipes and plans remain personal data.

## Current state

- TypeScript, React, TanStack Start and Vite run on Node. Recipes use a local JSON catalogue and optional product suggestions a local price snapshot; preferences and plans use browser storage. A fresh checkout starts empty. Cloudflare Workers/D1 remain a future direction.
- Recipes are exclusively AI-generated. Explicit week creation uses the installation owner's ChatGPT subscription through OAuth and tool-free Responses requests. Each clone connects its own account with `pnpm chatgpt:login`; credentials remain outside the repository. Recipes are marked untested until cooked; page loads never generate them. Recipe chat and revision requests have been removed; existing recipes and revision history remain readable.
- Code calculates per-serving macros from 2,567 BLS 4.0 groceries as bought; ready meals, drinks and cooked variants are excluded, and BLS lists no dried spices beyond pepper, basil, parsley and chives, so other herbs and spices count as zero. Food lookup takes two steps: the recipe call names each ingredient's food in search terms, code shortlists 8 BLS foods per ingredient, and a small second call picks one. The model also estimates edible weights for pieces and volumes. Missing matches, weights or nutrient values prevent a complete total; dietary and allergen labels remain provisional.
- Setup is four short screens: supermarket → preferences → pantry → schedule. The result has only Week and Shopping. There is no recipe browser, imagery or recipe chat; opening a planned meal shows its ingredients, method and per-serving nutrition.
- The five main preferences are One pot, High protein (at least 25 g per serving), Keto (at most 10 g carbs per serving), Low calorie (at most 500 kcal per serving) and Meal prep. Nutrition thresholds are checked against calculated values; missing macros do not qualify. Diet, allergens and ingredients to avoid remain in a disclosure. Keto is a per-meal carbohydrate filter, not a daily ketogenic plan.
- Creating a week generates it live: one AI call plans a varied menu, then exactly one recipe per scheduled meal is written, three at a time. Each recipe fills its meal as soon as it is saved; code rejects recipes that miss the preferences. Week shows servings, time, calories and protein per meal. Swaps use compatible saved recipes; no extra alternatives are generated.
- Creating a new week replaces the current plan once its menu exists; editing preferences or pantry keeps it. Without a ChatGPT connection, weekly assignment uses saved recipes, respects preferences and meal types, and leaves unsupported slots unfilled.
- Week offers **Regenerate week** with the current store, preferences, pantry and schedule, and **Start over** through all four setup screens from defaults. Restart progress survives reload; Cancel or Back to week restores the previous choices and shopping checks. Submitting the last screen commits the new choices; a menu failure still preserves the old meals and offers retry or saved recipes. Neither action deletes saved recipes.
- Pantry begins with named staples and lets users search ingredients from saved recipes. Olivenöl and Rapsöl replace the ambiguous olive-oil/generic-oil pair; an already selected generic oil remains labelled Öl (unspecified). Different oils are not silently substituted. Pantry records presence, not remaining quantity.
- Shopping combines compatible generic ingredients, scales quantities and excludes selected pantry staples. **Products & prices** is optional and collapsed initially. With a local product snapshot (`pnpm products:import`), opening it loads saved matches; **Find BILLA products** (named after the selected store) explicitly matches the rest. One AI call writes shop search terms, code shortlists 10 products per ingredient, and a second call picks suitable products. **Choose products by** (Cheapest, Store brand first, Organic first) and a per-ingredient swap select one product line per ingredient, with packs, cost and a "check amount" flag computed in code. The list shows an approximate cost and how many ingredients it covers, leaves products sold by weight without a comparable amount out of that total, and notes the snapshot date, stale prices and stores without data. Copy includes product lines only while the disclosure is open. Stock is never checked.
- Light and dark themes follow the device preference until changed using the header switch. The explicit choice is saved with the browser plan and restored before page content renders.
- The interface and recipe prose are English. Generic German/Austrian grocery names are intentional. No multilingual framework is planned; shopping items stay generic, and brands appear only in the optional product suggestions.
- The public repository excludes personal data, credentials, local agent/editor configuration, product data and development evidence. It includes no supermarket logos.

## Next step

Use the four-screen setup, cook a generated meal and shop from its list. Record confusing choices and incorrect amounts, timing or instructions; prioritize changes supported by that experience.

Still deferred: nutrition for dried spices, locking favourite meals before a rebuild, generation that continues after the page closes, marking recipes cooked, recipe deletion, daily macro targets, stock checks, product data for HOFER, Lidl and PENNY, fresher SPAR prices, faster matching through the priority tier (uses more plan usage), account sync and a packaged homeserver deployment. Images and recipe chat are deliberately outside the simplified flow. Private hosting needs persistent catalogue/auth directories and private-network or reverse-proxy access control. Plans remain browser-specific.

Known product gaps: matches are AI picks from a 10-product shortlist and can miss (BILLA's Feldgurken never reach the Gurke shortlist). A saved "nothing fits" stays until its entry is removed from `matches.json` (plain Rote Rüben at BILLA settle there, because fresh beets never reach the shortlist). Cheapest ranks by cost, so a cheap flagged piece can win against a large gram amount; its line says "check amount" and the total runs low. Store-brand detection knows only BILLA and SPAR brand names. Spoons, pieces and to-taste amounts often cannot be compared with packs, so those lines say "check amount". Product links exist only for BILLA, and shelf prices can differ from the snapshot.

## Source decisions

- **Recipes:** generated on request using each installation owner's ChatGPT plan; no scraped recipe collection is included.
- **Nutrition:** Max Rubner-Institut (2025), [BLS 4.0](https://blsdb.de/download), CC BY 4.0. The included snapshot preserves missing values and records its attribution, selected food IDs and transformations in [data/nutrition-source.json](data/nutrition-source.json).
- **Products and prices:** an unofficial snapshot from [Heisse Preise](https://github.com/badlogic/heissepreise), which scrapes Austrian shop websites. `pnpm products:import` downloads it into ignored `data/products.local/`; it is never committed, served or redistributed. The 2026-10-24 import has current BILLA prices (10,597 products), SPAR prices last changed on 2026-09-27 (21,689 products, shown as possibly out of date) and no data for HOFER, Lidl or PENNY, whose lists stay generic. AI only picks among code-shortlisted products; packs, costs and totals are calculated in code, and availability is never checked.
- **Third-party material:** nutrition data keeps its own licence, and product data stays local; application code and documentation use MIT. See [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md).
